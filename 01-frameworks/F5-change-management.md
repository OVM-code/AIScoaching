# F5 — Change Management Playbook for AI Integration

Best-practice change management (Prosci/ADKAR for individuals, Kotter for organizational momentum) adapted to what is *specific* about AI change: identity threat, skill anxiety, trust in machine output, shadow usage, and continuous change (AI keeps evolving — this is not a one-off go-live).

## Backbone: ADKAR per affected group

Every affected persona must move through five states; diagnose which state each group is stuck in and apply the matching lever — never "more communication" by default.

| State | The person can say… | Primary levers | AI-specific notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A**wareness | "I know why we're doing this" | Sponsor comms, change story (D23) | Address the elephant immediately: "what does this mean for my job?" Silence = they assume the worst |
| **D**esire | "I choose to take part" | WIIFM per persona, manager conversations, incentives, involving them in design | Strongest lever: remove hated work first. Fear is rational — counter with the workforce commitments (§6), not slogans |
| **K**nowledge | "I know how" | Training (D26), champions, job aids | AI literacy is layered: everyone / power users / agent owners / leaders. Prompting is the smallest part — judging output is the skill |
| **A**bility | "I can do it in my real work" | Practice time, floor support, redesigned processes that *require* the new way | Schedule protected practice hours; ability ≠ a webinar. Watch for "secret double work" (doing it the old way too) |
| **R**einforcement | "It sticks" | Metrics, recognition, targets & bonus alignment, ratchet decisions | Retire the old way explicitly (turn off the old form/route). Celebrate usage stories monthly. Agents improve → re-train periodically |

## Momentum: Kotter moves mapped to the engagement

| Kotter step | Where in this system |
|---|---|
| 1 Urgency | Assessment readout (D7): pain quantified in their own numbers |
| 2 Guiding coalition | Sponsor + project lead + champions network (D27) + a respected skeptic |
| 3 Vision & strategy | Strategy-on-a-page (D8) + change story (D23) |
| 4 Communicate | Persona comms calendar (D25); leaders repeat the story 7× more than feels natural |
| 5 Remove obstacles | Policy that *enables* (D29), licenses for everyone trained, time budget, fix the blocking data/process issues |
| 6 Short-term wins | Wave-1 pilots chosen partly FOR visibility (F3 rule); publicize scorecards |
| 7 Consolidate | Scale decisions at G4; org redesign locks in the new way (F4) |
| 8 Anchor | Governance (D30), targets/bonuses updated, onboarding includes AI ways-of-working |

## What's different about AI change (design for these)

1. **Identity threat, not just skill gap.** An estimator who prides herself on quoting is being told a machine drafts quotes. Response: reframe roles around the judgment that remains (F4 role cards do this explicitly), and let experts *train* the agent — being the teacher preserves status.
2. **Trust calibration.** People either over-trust (paste hallucinated output) or under-trust (ignore the tool). Training must include failure examples and verification habits; autonomy tiers (F4) make trust graduated and visible.
3. **Shadow AI.** A chunk of staff already uses ChatGPT privately. Never open with a ban: survey it (anonymously), legalize the safe part via policy + sanctioned tools, and recruit the heaviest users as champions.
4. **Continuous change.** Models and agents improve monthly. Build a rhythm (monthly AI hour, champion syncs) rather than a one-off campaign.
5. **Middle managers are the fulcrum.** They fear span-of-control loss and are the ones who must reinforce daily. Involve them in the org design (P3 workshops), give them the story first, and give them a visible role in pilots. If middle management is bypassed, adoption dies in month 3.

## Personas & WIIFM (default set — tailor per client)

| Persona | Typical fear | WIIFM to make true and tell |
|---|---|---|
| Field/frontline worker | "Big brother / I'm not a computer person" | Less paperwork after each job; help is one voice-note away |
| Back-office/admin | "This automates *me*" | The chase-and-retype work goes; the judgment and customer contact stays; new skills paid for |
| Sales/estimator | "My craft is being commoditized" | Quote in hours not days → higher win rate → higher bonus |
| Middle manager | "Fewer people to manage = less status" | From information-pusher to coach; exceptions and improvement become the job |
| Exec | "Money pit / compliance risk" | Gate metrics, kill criteria, governance from day one |
| IT | "Shadow tools, security holes, extra workload" | Seat at the table from P1; policy gives them control points; CoE/platform role |

## Resistance management

Diagnose before medicating: resistance is **information**. Sources in AI programs, most common first: fear of job loss → skill anxiety → loss of autonomy/status → distrust of output quality → past change fatigue → genuine flaw detection (listen to this one — skeptics find real edge cases; put them on the pilot team).

Escalation ladder: listen 1:1 → involve in design → address the real blocker → set expectations with consequences (last resort, sponsor's job, only after the first three).

## §6 Workforce messaging rules (do not improvise)

1. Leadership aligns on the true people-story **before** any broad announcement: growth-without-hiring? redeployment? possible reductions? One version of the truth.
2. If reductions are possible, say the process and timeline, not false reassurance ("no decisions before X; here's how decisions will be made; here's what we commit to: training first, redeployment first").
3. Sequence: sponsor → managers (with Q&A prep) → affected teams (face-to-face) → everyone else — within days, not weeks.
4. Works council/union informed per legal duty **before** rumor mills force it (BE/FR specifics in F4 §6).
5. Every commitment made is tracked in the change plan and reported on. Broken promises cost more than the program.

## Measurement (adoption dashboard, monthly)

- **Usage**: active users/licenses, agent runs, % of process volume through the new path
- **Proficiency**: training completion, certification passes, quality-of-output samples
- **Sentiment**: 3-question pulse (safe? useful? supported?) — trend matters, not level
- **Behavior**: old-way volume (should → 0), exception queue health, champion activity
- **Outcomes**: the pilot/benefit metrics (D22/D33) — the only slide the exec ultimately cares about
