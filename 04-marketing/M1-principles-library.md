# M1 — Principles Library (modular)

The distilled operating principles this go-to-market runs on. Each block: **ID · Principle · Source · Apply when · How it's applied here.** Cite block IDs in strategy/asset files; reuse the blocks for any future offer or niche.

> Sources are paraphrased from the thought leaders' published work (Hormozi *$100M Offers* / *$100M Leads*, Dunford *Obviously Awesome*, Miller *Building a StoryBrand*, Godin, Enns *Win Without Pitching Manifesto*, Stark, Weiss *Million Dollar Consulting*, Fields/Port and others). They're principles, not quotes.

---

## A. Offer design (Hormozi, *$100M Offers*)

**OFFER-1 · The Value Equation.** Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood of achievement) ÷ (Time delay × Effort & sacrifice). Raise the top, crush the bottom.
*Apply when:* designing or reviewing any offer, lead magnet, or even a post's promise.
*Here:* Kickstart promises a **business outcome** (hours back, growth without hiring — not "AI knowledge"), likelihood boosted by guarantee + case studies, time-delay crushed by "first working automation in week 3", effort crushed by done-with-you delivery (M2 §3).

**OFFER-2 · Grand Slam Offer.** Stack so much tangible value at an acceptable price that saying no feels stupid; sell in a category of one so there's no price comparison.
*Apply when:* packaging the core paid offer.
*Here:* the Kickstart bundles diagnosis + working automations + trained team + policy + 90-day support — positioned against "hiring an extra admin" and "a €15k consulting report", not against other AI trainers (M2 §3).

**OFFER-3 · Risk reversal / guarantee.** The guarantee is a sales asset, not fine print. Types: unconditional money-back, conditional ("if X not achieved, we work free until it is"), anti-guarantee (for high-touch: "if you do the work and see nothing, pay nothing"). Condition on client behaviors you need anyway.
*Apply when:* pricing resistance is fear-of-waste (SMEs: always).
*Here:* conditional guarantee tied to the scan's quantified opportunity (options in M2 §4).

**OFFER-4 · Price on value, premium.** Compete on price and you're a commodity; premium price funds over-delivery and makes clients take the work seriously ("those who pay, pay attention"). Never discount — add value or trade for something (case study, referral, speed).
*Apply when:* setting/negotiating fees.
*Here:* founder-pricing for the first 3 clients is **traded** for a documented case study + video testimonial + 2 introductions — never framed as a discount (M3 week 4+).

**OFFER-5 · Naming: M-A-G-I-C.** Magnet (reason/hook) – Avatar (who) – Goal (outcome) – Interval (time) – Container (format). Names sell before copy does.
*Apply when:* naming offers and lead magnets.
*Here:* "**De AI-Voorsprong** — voor installatie- en servicebedrijven: 10 uur per week terug in 6 weken (het AI Kickstart-traject)".

## B. Lead generation (Hormozi, *$100M Leads*)

**LEADS-1 · Give away the secrets, sell the implementation.** Free content should be genuinely usable; the offer sells speed, certainty, and done-with-you execution.
*Apply when:* deciding how much to reveal. Answer: more than feels comfortable.
*Here:* the AI-Scan lead magnet contains real use cases with real numbers; content shows actual builds (M4).

**LEADS-2 · Lead magnet rules.** Solve one narrow problem *completely*, for the exact avatar; make it fast to consume; solving it should *reveal* the bigger problem your paid offer solves; deliver it with a clear next step.
*Apply when:* creating any lead magnet.
*Here:* the AI-Scan lets an owner self-diagnose in 10 minutes (narrow problem: "where do I even start?") — and the score reveals the bigger problem (unstructured adoption = zero value), whose fix is the Kickstart.

**LEADS-3 · The Core Four.** Only four ways to get clients: (1) warm outreach, (2) posting content, (3) cold outreach, (4) paid ads. Start with warm outreach — it's free, fast, and high-trust. Do it *to exhaustion* before scaling the others. Advertise the lead magnet, not the offer.
*Apply when:* allocating weekly effort.
*Here:* M3 sequences warm-first (100 contacts), content in parallel for compounding, cold outreach from week 4, ads not before month 4 (if ever — this is a high-ticket local trust business).

**LEADS-4 · Rule of 100.** 100 primary actions per day (or per week for high-touch B2B: calibrate) — 100 warm messages, or 100 minutes of content, sustained. Volume beats cleverness; most people quit at 20% of sufficient volume.
*Apply when:* weekly planning; when "it's not working" (check volume first).
*Here:* M3's scorecard: 10 outreach touches/day, 3 posts + 20 meaningful comments/week.

**LEADS-5 · A-C-A warm outreach.** Acknowledge (something true about them) → Compliment (specific) → Ask (a question, not a pitch). Lead with interest, offer value, never open with the sale. "Give, give, give — until they ask."
*Apply when:* every warm/cold message.
*Here:* all scripts in `assets/outreach-scripts.md` follow A-C-A; the "give" is a free personalized AI-Scan debrief.

**LEADS-6 · Engaged-lead math.** Work backwards: 1 client ≈ 2-3 offers ≈ 4-6 discovery calls ≈ 10-15 scan debriefs ≈ 150-300 outreach touches / content-driven leads. Track the ratios, fix the weakest stage.
*Apply when:* setting activity targets and diagnosing the funnel.
*Here:* M3 §metrics.

## C. Positioning (Dunford · Godin · Enns)

**POS-1 · Positioning is context-setting (Dunford).** Define: competitive alternatives (what they'd do without you — usually "do nothing" or "hire an admin"), your unique attributes, the value those enable, who cares most, and the market category you enter.
*Apply when:* before any copy; when messaging feels generic.
*Here:* M2 §1-2. Category chosen: **AI-integration coach for home-services businesses** — not "AI consultant" (crowded, vague), not "AI trainer" (commodity).

**POS-2 · Smallest viable audience (Godin).** Pick the smallest group you can serve so well they'd miss you. Specificity buys word-of-mouth; trades talk to each other (sector federations, WhatsApp groups, suppliers' counters).
*Apply when:* tempted to widen the target "to not miss opportunities".
*Here:* Flemish installation/service trades first. Econocom-type corporates come via a *different* motion (authority + referral), not via broadening the public message.

**POS-3 · Specialize or be compared (Enns).** The vertical+discipline specialist skips the pitch: prospects come pre-sold by expertise signals. Never give away strategy in proposals; diagnose (paid or structured) before prescribing.
*Apply when:* someone asks for a free plan/proposal.
*Here:* the free 20-min debrief interprets *their scan*, it does not design solutions; design work starts at S1 of the paid Kickstart. Proposals are one page: outcome, plan reference, price, guarantee.

## D. Message (Miller, *StoryBrand*)

**MSG-1 · The client is the hero; you are the guide.** Show empathy ("I know what the evening paperwork does to a family business") + authority (method, cases, the system). Give a 3-step plan, a clear CTA, stakes (what staying stuck costs), and a picture of success.
*Apply when:* writing the landing page, the about section, talks.
*Here:* landing-page copy is structured exactly on this arc; content never says "look how smart AI is", always "here's the owner's Tuesday evening, before and after".

**MSG-2 · Clarity beats cleverness.** If the reader must think to understand what you do and what to do next, you lose. One offer, one CTA per asset.
*Apply when:* editing everything.
*Here:* every asset ends with exactly one CTA: "Doe de AI-Scan" (top of funnel) or "Boek de gratis debrief" (mid-funnel).

## E. Consulting business model (Stark · Weiss · Enns)

**BIZ-1 · Productize the entry point (Stark).** Fixed scope, fixed price, fixed duration, named deliverables — removes the two biggest buyer fears (open-ended cost, vague outcome) and makes the service referable ("book her Kickstart").
*Here:* the Kickstart IS the productized service (built in `03-clients/sme-home-services/`); the 7-phase engagement is the custom back-end.

**BIZ-2 · Value-based fees, not day rates (Weiss).** Anchor to the value created (hours × loaded cost, growth unlocked), never to your time. The scan/business-case numbers are the anchor.
*Here:* discovery-call script presents price *after* the quantified opportunity, as a fraction of year-1 value.

**BIZ-3 · The case-study flywheel.** First clients are marketing assets: founder pricing traded for documented before/after numbers, testimonial, referrals (OFFER-4). One great local case outperforms months of content in a trade niche.
*Here:* M3 makes the first-client case study a named deliverable of the engagement itself.

**BIZ-4 · Sell the vacation, not the flight (Hormozi/CLOSER).** People buy the destination (calm Tuesday evenings, growth without hiring), not the plane (LLMs, automations, org charts). Method earns *trust*, outcome earns the *yes*.
*Here:* discovery-call script and all copy lead with outcomes; the methodology appears as proof of likelihood (OFFER-1), one layer down.

**BIZ-5 · Document, don't just declare (show-your-work school).** Building in public — real builds, real numbers, lessons from real engagements (anonymized) — is the fastest authority engine for a solo expert; it converts skeptical trade audiences who smell hype instantly.
*Here:* content pillar #1 in M4 is "the build log".

## F. Using the library

- **Designing something new?** Walk OFFER-1 → LEADS-2 → POS-1 → MSG-1 in that order.
- **Something underperforming?** Diagnose in this order: volume (LEADS-4) → offer (OFFER-1/2) → message (MSG-1/2) → positioning (POS-1/2). It's almost never the logo.
- **New niche?** Re-run M2 §6's niche-swap prompt; the principle blocks stay, only their applications change.
